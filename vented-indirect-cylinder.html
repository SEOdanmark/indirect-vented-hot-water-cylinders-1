Flexiheat UK supplies a comprehensive range of indirect vented hot water cylinders for domestic, commercial and industrial hot water applications throughout the UK. Designed around the established principles of gravity-fed hot water storage, an indirect vented hot water cylinder provides stored domestic hot water while receiving its primary heating energy from an external heat source. Depending on the installation, this heat source can include a gas boiler, oil boiler, solid fuel boiler, heat pump system or solar thermal system. Heat is transferred to the stored potable water through an internal heat exchanger coil, keeping the heating circuit separate from the domestic hot water supply.

A property with several bathrooms, frequent simultaneous hot water use or a larger number of occupants may have different requirements from a smaller home. Selecting an appropriate indirect vented hot water cylinder therefore requires consideration of both stored water volume and the rate at which the external heat source can reheat the cylinder.

Flexiheat UK manufactures its indirect vented hot water cylinders from high-quality duplex stainless steel. Stainless steel has become an increasingly popular material for hot water cylinders and provides a modern alternative to traditional copper construction. Duplex stainless steel offers durability, good corrosion resistance and higher pressure capabilities than many conventional copper vented cylinders.

When talking about indirect vented hot water cylinders, one of the main differences compared with unvented cylinders is how the water supply and pressure are provided. A vented indirect hot water cylinder normally receives cold water from a cold water storage tank located above the cylinder, commonly in the loft. Gravity moves water from the cold water tank into the indirect vented cylinder. The height difference between the cold water storage tank and the hot water cylinder creates the static head that determines the available hot water pressure.

The maximum operating temperature of the stainless steel cylinder is 95°C. Inside the indirect vented hot water cylinder, the heat exchanger coil is manufactured from 316 stainless steel. These indirect coils have a maximum operating temperature of 120°C and a maximum working pressure of 10 bar. The construction provides a durable heat transfer arrangement for compatible external heating systems.

Flexiheat UK states that copper cylinders typically have maximum working heads of approximately 10 to 25 metres, equivalent to around 1.0 to 2.5 bar depending on copper grade and cylinder capacity. By comparison, the Flexiheat UK stainless steel vented cylinder range is rated to a maximum working pressure of 6 bar.

Indirect vented hot water cylinders continue to provide an effective solution for many UK properties, particularly homes with an existing open vented heating arrangement. A vented indirect hot water cylinder is normally supplied with cold water from a cold water storage cistern positioned above the cylinder. In many traditional UK properties, this cold water storage tank is installed in the loft. Gravity moves water from the cold water tank into the indirect vented cylinder and creates the pressure required to deliver hot water to taps, baths and other outlets.

Choosing between the available indirect vented hot water cylinders should ultimately be based on the requirements of the complete heating and domestic hot water system. The correct cylinder should provide sufficient stored water, suitable heat exchanger performance, appropriate immersion heater capacity where required and compatibility with the available static head and heat source.

An indirect vented cylinder is generally simpler than an unvented cylinder because it does not depend on a sealed mains-pressure system with the additional safety components associated with unvented installations. Where the property already has the necessary loft tank and open vent arrangement, an indirect vented hot water cylinder can consequently provide a straightforward solution.
